The benchmark shows that first-fit was faster than best-fit, taking 0.461628 seconds compared to 0.551499 seconds. This is because first-fit selects the first suitable free block, while best-fit searches for the smallest suitable block. Best-fit used a smaller heap of 1,310,720 bytes compared to first-fit's 1,376,256 bytes. However, best-fit recorded higher external fragmentation at 76.30%, compared to 57.71% for first-fit. This indicates that choosing smaller fitting blocks does not always produce better memory layout. First-fit left a larger largest-free block of 142,208 bytes, while best-fit left 64,384 bytes. Standard C malloc/free was significantly faster, completing the workload in 0.007704 seconds. However, its internal heap statistics were not measured by this benchmark. Both custom allocation policies passed heap consistency and cleanup checks. Overall, first-fit provided better execution speed and lower fragmentation in this workload, while best-fit used less total heap memory.
